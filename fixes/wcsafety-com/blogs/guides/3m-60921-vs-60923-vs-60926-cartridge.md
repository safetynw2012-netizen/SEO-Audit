## TL;DR

All three are 3M bayonet combination cartridges with a built-in P100 particulate filter. They differ in **gas/vapor scope**:

- **60921** — Organic vapor only.
- **60923** — Organic vapor + acid gas (HCl, SO₂, Cl, HF).
- **60926** — Multi-gas: OV + acid gas + ammonia/methylamine + formaldehyde + hydrogen sulfide. The broadest of the three.

Default rule of thumb when monitoring is ambiguous: pick the cartridge whose NIOSH-approved gas class covers everything the SDS or air-monitoring data even *hints* at. The cost difference is small; the protection difference is binary.

## When to pick each

### Pick the 60921 if…
- The work is pure organic-vapor exposure with particulates (spray painting, woodworking with solvent finishes, auto body refinish in a controlled booth).
- Acid gases and ammonia compounds are confirmed absent by air monitoring or SDS review.
- You want the lowest-cost combination cartridge that still gives you P100.

### Pick the 60923 if…
- Acid gases (HCl, SO₂, Cl, HF) are present, expected, or not ruled out.
- The work is in chemical processing, water treatment, metal pickling, semiconductor fabrication, or pulp & paper.
- Ammonia is documented absent.

### Pick the 60926 if…
- The hazard mix includes ammonia, methylamine, or formaldehyde — neither the 60921 nor 60923 covers these.
- Multiple gas classes are present or the exposure profile is uncertain.
- You're standardizing one cartridge across mixed work areas to simplify your respiratory-protection program.
