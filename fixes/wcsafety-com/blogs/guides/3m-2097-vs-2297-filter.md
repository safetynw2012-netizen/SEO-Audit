## TL;DR

Both the **3M 2097** and **3M 2297** are NIOSH P100 bayonet-mount filters with an activated-carbon layer that provides **nuisance-level relief from organic vapors** — odors and irritants below the OSHA PEL. Both filter 99.97% of oil-resistant particulates. They are not OV cartridges and are **not approved for OV exposures at or above the PEL**.

The 2097 and 2297 are close substitutes; the operational differences come down to current 3M product positioning and minor design updates. **`[VERIFY current product positioning and any spec deltas against 3M's TDS — 3M revises this lineup periodically.]`** If your facility already standardizes on one, stay there; if you're specifying fresh, the 2297 is 3M's more recent designation in this niche.

## What each filter is

### 3M 2097 — P100 with nuisance OV relief
- **NIOSH approval:** P100 (99.97%). `[VERIFY TC- approval number against NIOSH CEL]`
- Filter element: P100 mechanical layer + activated-carbon layer for nuisance OV / odor relief.
- **Not** a replacement for an OV cartridge when vapors are at or above the PEL.
- Bayonet mount.
- Typical jobs: welding fume + light solvent odors, grinding/sanding around solvent-cleaned surfaces, woodworking with low-residual finish off-gassing.

### 3M 2297 — P100 with nuisance OV relief

## Which to choose

**They're close substitutes.** If your facility already stocks one, stay there for procurement simplicity. If you're specifying fresh, the **2297** is 3M's more recently positioned filter in this category — confirm against current 3M product availability.

**Critical reminder for both:** the activated-carbon layer is only rated for *nuisance-level* OV. If air monitoring shows organic vapors at or above the OSHA PEL, you need a proper OV cartridge (6001, 6003, 60921, 60923, or 60926) — not the 2097 or 2297.

## Compatibility

Both use the 3M bayonet mount and fit the 6000 / 6500 / 7500 half facepieces and 6800 / 6900 full facepieces.
