## What this page covers

WC Safety publishes buying guides and product reviews across respiratory protection, head and face protection, hand and eye protection, smoke and carbon-monoxide detection, generators, and emergency preparedness. This page documents how every one of those articles is researched, tested where possible, written, fact-checked, and updated.

## Who writes our content

Every article is written by a named editor whose byline and short bio appear at the top of the article. Editors are recruited for one or more of:

- Direct trades or industrial experience (construction, manufacturing, lab, fire/EMS, military).
- Formal certifications relevant to the topic — examples we look for include OSHA 30-hour General Industry or Construction, NIOSH-approved respirator fit-test training, CSP (Certified Safety Professional), or CIH (Certified Industrial Hygienist).
- Journalism experience in safety, trades, or consumer-product reporting.

Articles touching on respiratory protection, electrical safety, or life-safety detection are additionally reviewed by [Reviewer Name, credential] before publication.

## The five things every product must satisfy

Before a product can appear in a "Best…" ranking on WC Safety, it must:

1. **Meet the applicable US safety standard.** We list the standard explicitly in the article (for example: NIOSH 42 CFR 84 for respirators, ANSI/ISEA Z89.1 for hard hats, UL 217 for smoke alarms, UL 2034 for CO alarms, ANSI Z87.1 for safety eyewear, NFPA 1981 where relevant). Products without certification — or with self-claimed but unverifiable certification — are excluded.
2. **Be available from at least one US retailer** within the last 30 days at the time of writing or last update.
3. **Have been hands-on inspected, hands-on used, or specification-and-review compared** — and the basis is stated. We do not write "we tested" if we did not.
4. **Have a documented manufacturer behind it.** Anonymous-brand respirators and detectors are excluded regardless of price or review count.
5. **Have a clearly documented warranty or return path** from either the manufacturer or the retailer.

## How testing actually works

Our reviewing falls into three labelled tiers. Every article tells you which tier applies:

- **Hands-on tested.** A team member has used the product in its intended environment for at least [X hours / Y shifts / Z fit-test cycles]. Notes are kept in our internal test log. Photos accompany the review.
- **Bench inspected.** A team member has unboxed and physically examined the product but has not used it long enough to assess durability. Suitable for items where critical attributes (fit, build quality, labelling) are verifiable on inspection.
- **Spec compared.** No hands-on inspection. The ranking is based on manufacturer datasheets, NIOSH/OSHA certification databases, retailer specifications, and aggregated verified-purchase reviews. We label these "based on documented specifications and reviews."

A single "Best…" guide may mix tiers — for example, the editor may have hands-on tested the top three picks and spec-compared the rest. The label always appears next to each product.

## Sources we consult

For every safety guide we cross-check at least three of the following:

- The NIOSH Certified Equipment List (CEL) for respirator approvals.
- OSHA standards and letters of interpretation for workplace requirements.
- Underwriters Laboratories (UL) certification listings for detectors and fire equipment.
- ANSI/ISEA standards for PPE.
- Manufacturer specification sheets and user manuals.
- FEMA and Red Cross preparedness guidelines for emergency-kit content.
- Independent third-party reviews from established publications (Consumer Reports, Wirecutter, This Old House, etc.) where applicable — cited inline.
- Aggregated verified-purchase reviews on Amazon, Home Depot, and Grainger.

We do not cite manufacturer marketing claims as fact. If a claim cannot be verified, we say "the manufacturer states" and link to the source.

## How we handle disagreements with manufacturers

If a manufacturer disputes something we've written, we ask for documentation. If the documentation is convincing we update the article (with a "Last updated" timestamp and a brief change note). If it is not, we leave the article unchanged. Manufacturers cannot pay to change a review and cannot pay to remove one.

## How we keep articles current

- "Best of [year]" articles are reviewed and updated **every January**. If a product is discontinued, recalled, or fails a new standard, it is removed mid-cycle.
- We monitor [CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls) and manufacturer recall feeds. Recalled products are removed from any active ranking within seven days of public notice, with an editor's note explaining why.
- Every page carries a visible "Last updated" date.
